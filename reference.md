# Tailor Application — question bank

Draw from this in Phase 3. **Ask only what the gap ledger classes as ASKABLE.**
Never ask a STRUCTURAL row (no answer can close it) or a RESEARCHABLE one (you can
answer it yourself). A question whose answer already sits in the base résumé, the
profile note, or the posting is a question that makes the interview feel like a form.

Organised by the kind of gap, not by topic — so you can go straight from a ledger row
to the right question.

---

## Gap type 1 — The target wants evidence the base docs don't show

The most common gap. The posting or values page names something real, and the base
documents have nothing mapped to it.

- Where have you done <requirement> before, even outside work — school, club, a
  project, a side thing?
- The posting wants <skill>. What's the closest thing you've actually done? Walk me
  through one specific instance, start to finish.
- Has anyone ever relied on you for <responsibility>? What happened?
- What's the largest <number-bearing thing> you've handled — dollar amount, headcount,
  hours, accounts, transactions?
- Is there something you did that you cut from the résumé for space that speaks to this?

## Gap type 2 — A bullet is true but vague

The evidence exists but is unusable as written. Push once for the number and the
situation.

- "<vague bullet>" — what was the situation, what did you actually do, and what changed
  because you did it?
- How many / how much / over what period?
- What would have happened if you hadn't done it?
- Who noticed? Did it change how anyone worked afterward?
- Was that a one-off or something you ran repeatedly?

## Gap type 3 — Motivation for *this* target is unknown

Cover letters die here. The hook must be real, and only Braxton can supply the
genuine version.

- Why this company over the other three you could apply to in the same cycle?
- Why this office specifically — anything connecting you to that city, that market,
  that client base?
- Do you know anyone there, or has anyone from there spoken at SFU/UBC? Any info
  session, coffee chat, or campus event?
- What about <line of business> interests you more than <the adjacent one>?
- Research turned up <sourced fact>. Does that actually interest you, or should I find
  a different hook?
- What do you want to be doing two years after this role?

## Gap type 4 — A must-have is genuinely unmet

Decide together whether to address it, route around it, or accept it.

- <Requirement> is a stated must-have and you don't have it. Are you in progress on it,
  or is it a hard gap?
- Would you commit to a timeline for it in the letter, or leave it out?
- Is there an equivalent you'd argue substitutes for it?
- Should the letter address this head-on, or stay silent and let the résumé speak?

## Gap type 5 — Positioning forks

Use **AskUserQuestion** for these — they're choices, not recall.

- Which do you want leading the résumé: <experience A> or <experience B>?
- Register: plainly professional, warm and personable, or technically precise?
- Lead the letter on the sourced office fact, on the line of business, or on a
  personal connection?
- Emphasise breadth (lots of roles) or depth (one role, done seriously)?
- Are you positioning as a finance specialist or a well-rounded generalist for this one?

## Gap type 6 — Logistics the posting needs and the docs don't state

Quick, factual, usually one round.

- Start date and availability, and any hard constraints (course load, exam period)?
- Term length — 4, 8, 12, 16 months?
- Can you work in that city — commuting, relocating, or already there?
- Work authorisation, if the posting asks?
- Deadline you're working to, and is there an earlier internal one?
- Do they want a transcript, references, or a portfolio alongside?

## Gap type 7 — Contradictions

Ask when research and the documents disagree. These need resolving before writing.

- The base résumé says <X> but the profile note says <Y>. Which is current?
- The posting is listed in <city> but the office research points to <other city>. Which
  are you applying to?
- You've listed <interest> here, but this role is squarely <other thing>. Is the
  interest still live, or is this a deliberate pivot?

---

## Round-running notes

- **~4–6 per round.** Group by gap type so the round has one theme.
- **Re-derive the ledger after each round** — good answers often close gaps you hadn't
  asked about yet, and open new ones.
- **Two to three rounds is typical.** More when the base documents were written for a
  very different kind of role.
- **Push once, then move on.** One follow-up on a thin answer; don't interrogate.
- **Never ask for something you can look up.** Company facts are your job, not his.
- **Confirm inferences.** "I'm assuming you'd relocate for this — correct?" beats
  silently writing it in.
- **Stop** when every ASKABLE row is closed, or he says to write it. STRUCTURAL rows go
  straight to the gap list and never hold up the writing.

## Anti-patterns

- Asking a fixed list regardless of the target — the ledger exists to prevent this.
- Asking about something already in the base résumé.
- Asking a question whose only purpose is to generate a sentence you could omit.
- Continuing to interview after he's said "just write it."

# Fit score rubric

Referenced from Phase 4. Show the arithmetic — an unexplained percentage is decoration.

## Method

1. List the posting's **must-haves** and **nice-to-haves** separately. If the posting
   doesn't split them, treat anything phrased as "required / minimum / must" as a
   must-have and the rest as nice-to-have.
2. Score each row:
   - **1.0 — MET.** Direct, demonstrable evidence in the base docs or profile note.
   - **0.5 — PARTIAL.** Adjacent evidence that needs framing (student leadership for
     "team management"; coursework for "financial analysis").
   - **0.0 — GAP.** Nothing honest maps to it.
3. Weight: **must-haves ×3, nice-to-haves ×1.**
4. `fit% = (Σ weighted score ÷ Σ maximum possible) × 100`, rounded to the nearest 5.

## Worked example — RBC Banking Advisor Intern

| Requirement | Type | Score | Weighted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client-facing / sales experience | must | 0.5 | 1.5 / 3 |
| Enrolled in a business degree | must | 1.0 | 3 / 3 |
| Strong communication | must | 1.0 | 3 / 3 |
| CSC or IFIC | must | 0.0 | 0 / 3 |
| Cash-handling accuracy | nice | 0.5 | 0.5 / 1 |
| Second language | nice | 0.0 | 0 / 1 |

Σ weighted = 8.0 · Σ max = 14 → **8.0 ÷ 14 = 57% → 55%**

Reported as: *"55% — strong on the degree and communication must-haves, but the CSC is
unmet and costs 3 of the 14 available points on its own. Closing it moves this to 75%."*

## Honesty rules

- **Never round up past a 5-point bucket** to make a number look better.
- A 0.0 on a must-have must be **named in the summary**, not buried in the table.
- If fit lands **under 40%**, say so plainly and ask whether it's still worth applying —
  it often is for a campus cycle, but that's Braxton's call to make with the real number
  in front of him, not a number massaged to feel encouraging.
- The score describes *the match*, not *the person*. Phrase it that way.

# ATS keyword pass

Big 4, bank and large-employer co-op applications are machine-screened before a human
reads them. This pass is mechanical, not stylistic.

## Method

1. **Extract the posting's exact noun phrases** — the skills, tools, credentials and
   responsibilities, in their wording. "Client relationship management", not "client
   stuff". Keep their singular/plural and their word order.
2. **Diff against the tailored résumé.** For each phrase: present verbatim, present as a
   synonym, or absent.
3. **Convert honest synonyms to their wording.** If a bullet truthfully describes client
   relationship management, use that phrase. Machine matching is literal.
4. **Leave absent phrases absent.** A keyword with no true experience behind it is
   fabrication, and it fails at interview even when it passes the filter.
5. **Spell out then abbreviate** on first use — "Canadian Securities Course (CSC)" —
   since filters may match either form.

## Constraints

- **Truth outranks the filter, always.** Never add a keyword to pass a screen.
- No keyword stuffing, no white text, no hidden keyword blocks. These are detected, and
  they get applications discarded.
- Standard section headings ("Experience", "Education", "Skills") parse reliably;
  clever ones may not.
- Keep it readable by a human — the filter is the first reader, not the last.

# Worked gap ledger — full example

Target: **Deloitte, Audit & Assurance co-op, Vancouver office.**

| Target requirement / hook | Evidence in base docs | Status | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enrolled in accounting-track degree | SFU Beedie BBA, UBC Sauder transfer | MET | — |
| "Analytical mindset" | ECON 105, Investing Hub project | MET | — |
| Client service experience | Student council (27 members) | PARTIAL | ASKABLE |
| Experience with audit software | absent | GAP | STRUCTURAL |
| CPA prerequisite courses | unclear from base résumé | GAP | ASKABLE |
| Vancouver office = mid-market clients | absent | GAP | RESEARCHABLE |
| "Teamwork in a fast-paced setting" | absent | GAP | ASKABLE |
| Availability for Sept–Dec term | absent | GAP | ASKABLE |

**Round 1 asks four things** — the ASKABLE rows: a client-service instance, CPA
prerequisite standing, a fast-paced teamwork example, and term availability.

**It does not ask** about audit software (STRUCTURAL — goes to the gap list) or about
the Vancouver client base (RESEARCHABLE — that's the skill's job, not Braxton's).

That's the whole discipline: four questions, all of which only he can answer.
